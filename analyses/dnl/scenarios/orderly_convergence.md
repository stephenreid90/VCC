# DNL under Orderly Convergence

**Company:** Dyno Nobel Limited (ASX:DNL)
**Scenario:** Orderly Convergence (upside boundary)
**Version:** 2026-Q2-v1
**Type:** Per-scenario impact narrative per §16.1 item 4.

---

## The headline

Orderly Convergence is mildly positive for DNL but is not the strongest scenario among our three test companies. The mining-customer capex cycle is healthy under coordinated transition and broad-based recovery; transition-mineral demand (lithium, copper, nickel) is particularly supportive of the customer base; technology-product premium re-rates as growth-stock multiples expand. DNL benefits but is structurally a cyclical industrial — it doesn't get the multiple-expansion windfall that growth-multiple-quality stocks like CSL receive. The single scenario where every dimension resolves favourably for DNL, but the *degree* of upside is moderate not dramatic.

## How the impact matrix transmits

Seven drivers populated under Orderly Convergence, with the dominant moves on volume, equity risk premium, and terminal-state.

- **Volume growth — positive / moderate / moderate confidence.** Global mining-customer capex cycle healthy; transition-mineral demand particularly supportive. Volume growth runs at +2-4% vs Muddle Through baseline of ~1-3%.
- **Price/mix — positive / small / moderate.** Technology product mix lifts gradually under broader productivity acceleration in mining customer base; DigiShot electronic detonator share rises faster than baseline.
- **Gross margin — positive / small / moderate.** Input costs normalise; supply-chain disruption unwinds; modest margin uplift.
- **Risk-free rate — negative / small / moderate.** Real rates ease modestly from Muddle Through baseline (~1%) toward ~0.5-0.8%. Modest tailwind to valuation multiples.
- **Equity risk premium — negative / moderate / moderate.** Risk appetite recovers; cyclical industrial ERP compresses meaningfully toward long-run average. **One of the biggest valuation lifts for DNL** alongside the volume / margin gains.
- **Terminal growth rate — positive / small / low.** Modestly higher long-run growth; low confidence because terminal trajectory depends on African skills-development and migration-corridor progress that is presumed but not yet realised.
- **Terminal ROIC — positive / small / moderate**, with **defended exception** per §10.6 rule 2 (moat source = scale + switching cost + resource; decay horizon 10-15 years; named threat = Orica competitive response; sensitivity test = halving the fade-period extension preserves positive scenario value). Productivity surprise sustains excess returns marginally longer than baseline.

## DNL-specific divergence from the archetype matrix

DNL benefits from the archetype's pattern with minor variation:

1. **US cost-curve advantage** continues to deliver against the productive macro backdrop. Long-term gas contracts hold their value; the gas market normalises but DNL's contracted position is above-current-market regardless.
2. **Australian duopoly** preserves margin discipline. Coordinated transition supports rather than disrupts mining-customer demand.
3. **Technology pipeline accelerates** modestly as enterprise-AI adoption broadens (consistent with Orderly Convergence's AI-productivity-works narrative). DNL's DigiShot platform and AI-driven blast-design programs benefit from broader customer demand for productivity-enhancing services.

## Transmission channels for DNL

**Revenue:** healthy growth, ~3-5% per year vs Muddle Through ~1-3%. Volume + price/mix combine for modest revenue acceleration.

**Margin:** modest improvement. Gross margin into 30-33% range (vs 28-32% baseline); EBIT margin into 14-16% range (vs 13-15% baseline). Input cost normalisation contributes more than mix shift in the early years.

**Capex:** maintenance-led with modest growth tilt. Capital intensity ~6-7% of revenue. Some new capacity additions in transition-mineral-serving regions (Pilbara, Chile lithium-supporting areas) but constrained by management capital-discipline communication.

**Working capital:** stable.

**Financial / risk drivers:** real rates ease modestly; equity risk premium compresses; cost of debt eases modestly. Multiple expansion comes from both lower discount rates and modestly higher growth assumption. This is the dual-track lift that delivers the scenario's positive value.

**Terminal state:** terminal ROIC slightly above WACC for the fade period (defended exception); fade period extends modestly; terminal growth ~2.5-3% nominal USD (vs 2% baseline).

## Why DNL isn't the strongest beneficiary among our three test companies

DNL is fundamentally a cyclical industrial. Orderly Convergence is supportive of cyclical industrials (volume recovery, capex healthy, financial conditions easing) but the *valuation lift* is moderate because:

1. **No multiple-expansion windfall.** Growth-stock premium expansion accrues to CSL (specialty biologics, quality-growth profile), not to DNL. DNL trades on near-term EBITDA and asset-base multiples that move modestly with cycle.
2. **Cost advantage decays whether or not scenarios resolve well.** The US gas-contract roll-off in 2028-2030 is a function of contract terms, not macro. Orderly Convergence doesn't extend the contracts; it just makes the downstream economics more benign.
3. **Mining capex cyclicality has its own rhythm.** Mining majors' capex decisions depend on commodity price expectations + their own balance sheets + project pipeline maturity. Orderly Convergence helps but doesn't transform the cycle.

The comparison: **CSL** under Orderly Convergence likely re-rates upward materially via multiple expansion + earnings beat. **WBC** under Orderly Convergence benefits from credit-quality benignity + AI cost-to-income improvement. **DNL** under Orderly Convergence sees revenue / margin tailwinds and modest multiple expansion via lower ERP — supportive but not transformative.

## Indicative directional view on valuation

- **Valuation under Orderly Convergence sits perhaps 15-25% above Muddle Through.** The lift comes roughly equally from: (a) modestly higher near-term earnings (revenue / margin combined +5-10%), (b) modestly lower discount rate (ERP -50bps, real rates -20bps), and (c) slightly extended fade period and modestly higher terminal growth.
- **Asymmetry vs Muddle Through is moderate.** Less asymmetric than Stagflation downside or Disorderly Climate downside.

## What would shift DNL out of Orderly Convergence

- Continued sticky inflation despite supply normalisation → DNL moves back to Muddle Through.
- Inflation un-anchoring → Stagflation Persists.
- Persistent enterprise-AI adoption lag → AI Productivity Lag (no meaningful DNL impact).
- Geopolitical escalation → Fragmentation.

For DNL specifically, the **gas-contract reset timing** still bites regardless of scenario. Even Orderly Convergence doesn't extend the contracts — it just affects what the post-roll-off pricing environment looks like (better in Orderly than in Stagflation; better than in Fragmentation).
