# IPL under Disorderly Climate Crystallisation

**Company:** Incitec Pivot Limited (ASX:IPL)
**Scenario:** Disorderly Climate Crystallisation (climate boundary)
**Version:** 2026-Q2-v1
**Type:** Per-scenario impact narrative per §16.1 item 4.

---

## The headline

Disorderly Climate Crystallisation is **bifurcating** for IPL — material negative on near-term margins via carbon-cost flow-through plus coal-customer mix attrition, partial offset via transition-mineral demand acceleration. IPL is more exposed than the industrial-explosives archetype average due to its own ammonia self-supply (~55% of internal use, captured in the company-level `scenario_sensitivity_overrides_global.energy_transition.operational_carbon_exposure` override). The scenario is also where terminal-state assumptions get tested hardest — coal-customer infrastructure decays faster than the standard fade period assumes, and ammonia-production assets carry stranded-asset risk. Net outcome depends materially on customer-mix shift pace; on the framework's central assumption, the net for IPL is moderately negative.

## How the impact matrix transmits

Eleven drivers populated — the most heavily populated scenario for IPL alongside Stagflation.

- **Volume growth — negative / small / moderate.** Bifurcating mining-customer demand. Thermal coal mining declines materially; metallurgical coal continues; transition-mineral mining accelerates. Net modestly negative for aggregate volumes initially given coal-mining customer mix ~35%; recovers as transition-mineral demand compounds.
- **Price/mix — neutral / small / low.** Customer-mix shift toward transition minerals may modestly improve product mix but offset by margin pressure on cost pass-through.
- **Gross margin — negative / moderate / high.** Carbon-cost pass-through to ammonia production materially affects industrial explosives manufacturing margin. **IPL more exposed than archetype average** due to ammonia self-supply — captured in company-level override. Carbon prices spike to EUR 200+ in advanced economies; cross-border-adjustment forces pricing onto imports.
- **Input cost pass-through — negative / moderate / moderate.** Carbon-driven cost increases harder to pass through to mining customers themselves under cost pressure from their own carbon exposure. Asymmetric where coal-customer subsegment is most stressed.
- **Maintenance capex as % revenue — positive / moderate / moderate.** Decarbonisation capex for ammonia production (carbon capture, green ammonia pilots).
- **Growth capex as % revenue — positive / moderate / moderate.** Transition-aligned capacity additions (green ammonia, transition-mineral-mining service expansion).
- **Risk-free rate — positive / small / low.** Choppy rates with elevated volatility; central banks face policy dilemma. Modestly higher; low confidence reflects the volatility itself.
- **Equity risk premium — positive / moderate / moderate.** Sector dispersion wide. Industrial explosives broadly seen as carbon-exposed (mining customer base, own ammonia production); ERP premium added relative to transition-aligned sectors.
- **Terminal growth rate — negative / moderate / moderate.** Long-run growth shaded down via accelerated coal-mining customer attrition. Partial offset from transition-mineral mining but net negative.
- **Terminal ROIC — negative / moderate / moderate**, with **defended exception** per §10.6 rule 2: moat source = resource (long-term gas contracts) + scale; decay horizon 10-15 years (faster than non-climate scenarios); named threat = carbon pricing flow-through plus coal-customer attrition; sensitivity test = halving decay horizon to 5-7 years shows additional 15-20% terminal-value impact. **This is the scenario where terminal-state convergence discipline is tested hardest** for IPL.
- **Fade period length — negative / small / moderate.** Faster fade to convergence under transition pressures. Moat assets decay faster under stranded-asset and carbon-pricing dynamics.

## IPL-specific divergence from the archetype matrix

This is where the IPL company-level overrides become acutely relevant:

1. **Operational carbon exposure: HIGH** (vs archetype average). The ammonia self-supply position that's a *positive* under supply-chain stress (Fragmentation) becomes a *negative* under climate stress because IPL bears the carbon-cost flow-through directly rather than via supplier pricing. The Louisiana ammonia plant in particular faces direct carbon-pricing exposure.
2. **Coal-customer mix ~35%** is captured in the archetype-specific positioning block. This is the dominant transition-risk concentration. Within coal:
   - Thermal coal (~20% of mining customer mix) faces accelerated decline under Disorderly Climate.
   - Metallurgical coal (~15-20% of mining customer mix) faces moderate decline as green-steel pathways scale up but slower than thermal.
3. **Transition-mineral customer base offsets partially.** BHP nickel, FMG iron ore (for green steel feedstock), broader copper / lithium exposure across IPL's mining customer base provides demand pull on the other side. Speed of customer-mix shift is the key variable.
4. **US gas-contract position is a partial defence** — though carbon pricing may flow through gas costs as well via upstream carbon adjustment, the contract structure pre-dates the carbon-pricing regime and may include limited carbon pass-through. Legal/commercial uncertainty.

## Transmission channels for IPL

**Revenue:** modest near-term decline followed by recovery as transition-mineral demand compounds. Coal-customer revenue declines materially in years 2-5 of the scenario; transition-mineral revenue accelerates from year 3-4 onwards. Net trough probably 5-10% below baseline; recovery toward baseline by year 8-10.

**Margin:** moderately negative. Carbon-cost flow-through compresses gross margin 200-400bps depending on pricing power realised. Mining customers themselves under cost pressure resist pass-through.

**Capex:** materially elevated. Both maintenance (decarbonisation of existing ammonia capacity) and growth (transition-aligned capacity). Capital intensity rises 2-3pp from baseline.

**Working capital:** modestly higher.

**Financial / risk drivers:** ERP up moderately; rates volatile (net modestly higher); cost of debt up. Modest valuation multiple compression.

**Terminal state:** **the defining challenge of this scenario for IPL.** Terminal ROIC defended-exception case (moat decays faster). Terminal growth shaded down. Fade period shortened. The compound effect on terminal value is meaningful — possibly 20-30% terminal-value reduction depending on how the defended-exception is calibrated.

## The transition-mineral offset

Worth being explicit because it's the partial positive within a negative scenario:

- **Lithium** — Australian production scales significantly. IPL's Western Australian customer base benefits.
- **Copper** — global demand acceleration for electrification + grid investment. IPL's customer base across multiple geographies benefits.
- **Nickel** — battery-grade demand. Australian / Indonesian (Indonesian limited for IPL given regional alignment).
- **Rare earths** — strategic-mineral push. Australian production growth.
- **Iron ore (green-steel grade)** — high-grade iron ore for hydrogen-direct-reduced iron processes. FMG investment in green-steel pathways supports demand for blasting services in their Pilbara operations.

These offsets are real but cumulatively probably worth ~3-5% volume by year 5-7 of the scenario — not enough to fully offset the coal-customer decline plus carbon-cost margin compression.

## Stranded-asset risk and the terminal state

This is the scenario where the framework's §1 cost-of-capital convergence discipline gets its hardest test. Specifically for IPL:

- **Louisiana ammonia plant** — large, long-life, carbon-intensive. Direct exposure to US carbon pricing if implemented; embedded in cross-border-adjustment mechanism risk. Possible stranded-asset write-down inside scenario horizon.
- **Coal-customer-dedicated on-site infrastructure** — mobile manufacturing units stationed at coal mine sites. Useful life shortens as customer mines wind down. Re-deployment to transition-mineral sites possible but incurs cost.
- **Mining-services revenue base** — concentrated in customers whose own economics deteriorate. Default / contract renegotiation risk.

The terminal-state defended-exception attempts to capture this without claiming a permanent breach of convergence — moat decays faster, but converges to cost of capital over the (shortened) fade period.

## Indicative directional view on valuation

- **Valuation under Disorderly Climate sits perhaps 25-35% below Muddle Through.** Larger than Fragmentation, smaller than Stagflation. The compression is more terminal-loaded than near-term-loaded — the terminal-value reduction from faster moat decay is the single biggest driver, ahead of near-term margin compression.
- **Asymmetry within the scenario is wide.** Transition-mineral upside materialising faster reduces the negative materially; coal-customer attrition happening faster amplifies it.
- **Strategic-friend benchmark relevance.** If the strategist friend's view is bullish on transition-mineral offset (a plausible position for an Australian-listed mining-services name), the framework's view may sit below the strategist. If bearish on coal-customer wind-down speed, the framework may sit above. The divergence localisation per §15.1 would point to assumptions about customer-mix shift pace.

## What would shift IPL out of Disorderly Climate Crystallisation

- Continued drift without crystallisation → IPL moves back to Muddle Through (with Muddle Through's gradual climate-policy patchiness left in place).
- Major political reset enabling orderly transition → IPL toward Orderly Convergence.
- Climate-policy backsliding under macro crisis → IPL toward Stagflation's climate-stagnant posture.

For IPL specifically, the watchable variables are **carbon-pricing trajectory in US / Australia** (where IPL's ammonia plants sit) and **mining-customer-mix transition speed** (the volume side).
