# The resource_lifecycle overlay and the mining driver set

**Status: written 20 October 2026, first build for Evolution Mining (EVN); D-99 price paths revised the same afternoon after Stephen's review; D-104 cost-price link built the same evening. Serious.**
D-94 and D-95 are Stephen's rulings of 20 October. D-96 to D-101 are PROPOSED: they are
built so the result can be reviewed, and each one can be reversed without rewriting the
engine. Every number below comes from `design/methodology/resource_valuation_sets.yaml`
(set `evn`, status `current`), generated by `scripts/size_resource_valuation.py` and
asserted by `tests/dcf/test_resource_valuation_sets.py` (standing rule 4).

## 1. The problem in plain terms

The framework was built on three going concerns. Each has a capital base that persists,
revenue that grows from a base year, and a terminal that converges an excess return to
the cost of capital over a Five Forces decay band (D-62, D-71, D-86).

A gold miner breaks all three assumptions.

1. Volume is a mine plan, not a growth rate. Each mine produces at a planned rate and then
   stops when its ore runs out.
2. Price is set on a world market. A producer takes it. The price is a scenario input, not
   a company judgement.
3. The excess return is not protected by a barrier. It is the rent on ore the company owns.
   It ends when the ore ends, not when a rival competes it away.

Point 3 is the important one. D-43a already says a RENT has an end date and is carried in
the explicit period, while a BARRIER is what the decay horizon measures. An ore body is a
rent. So a miner has no Five Forces decay horizon. Its "decay" is depletion.

### D-96 (proposed). A fourth engine

`adding_a_company.md` says a company that fits none of the three engines needs a ruling
before code. EVN fits none, so `dcf/resource_engine.py` is proposed. It is a mine-by-mine
depletion DCF. It reuses the industrial engine's stub, mid-period discounting, single WACC
and equity bridge. It names no company and no mine.

### D-97 (proposed). How a finite mine life sits with D-71 and D-86

The proposal keeps D-71 as written and reads D-86 through D-43a.

1. **Ten explicit years stay (D-71).** The first ten years after the stub are shown and
   discounted exactly as for the other companies.
2. **The terminal is the depletion run-off.** After year ten, each mine keeps producing
   until its declared inventory is gone. The terminal value is the present value of those
   cash flows. D-71 already allows a multi-phase terminal where cash flows are not static
   at year ten; this is that case.
3. **Then nothing: replacement earns the cost of capital.** Once the ore is mined, new
   ounces come from exploration and acquisition at prices that earn about the WACC. Their
   NPV is zero, so the terminal carries no perpetuity. In D-62's terms, the capital base at
   closure is nil and N = 0.
4. **D-86's rule still holds, with the horizon set by ore.** The starting return is still
   the earned return. The fade length for a resource archetype is the remaining ore life,
   mine by mine, not a Five Forces band. The archetype says so in its `resource_lifecycle`
   block, and the company declares `no_barrier_declared` on its moat (D-43a: every source
   is a rent).

The alternatives, sized in §7: a reserves-only life (shorter) and a five-year life
extension per mine (a proxy for positive-NPV replacement). A perpetual going-concern
terminal is not proposed. It would assume a barrier the Five Forces work says does not
exist.

### D-98 (proposed). What counts as inventory, and what capital it carries

1. **Inventory basis: the company's stated mine life.** Each mine runs at its FY27 guidance
   midpoint from the valuation date to its disclosed life-of-mine year: Cowal 2042, Ernest
   Henry 2040, Northparkes 2054, Mungari 2038, Red Lake 2040 (Annual Report 2025).
   Reserves are struck at A$3,000/oz, about half today's AUD price, so a reserves-only life
   understates what the company plans to mine. Reserves-only and a five-year extension are
   the sensitivities.
2. **The ore drives the mine.** When the first of a mine's metals runs out, the mine stops.
3. **Excluded:** Mt Rawdon (finished September 2026), Marsden (undeveloped, no decision),
   Carnaby (a scheme vote is pending; an acquisition at zero NPV under D-97).
4. **Capital.** Sustaining capital and major mine development run at the FY27 rate per
   ounce to closure: they keep a mine producing. Major project capital runs off by a
   quarter a year from the FY27 midpoint to nil by FY31, when the named projects finish.
5. **Project uplifts.** The metal that project capital buys is credited where the company
   has quantified it: Bert (+12koz gold, +6kt copper a year for seven years from FY29) and
   the two Northparkes recovery projects (+4 points of gold recovery and +3 of copper from
   FY29, applied as a 4% and 3% lift in output, which is conservative). Northparkes E22 is
   charged but not credited: its volume is not quantified in the documents.

### D-102 (ruled 20 Oct 2026). Principles for commodity price paths

Pending better source data, every commodity path follows these rules.

1. **Start from the observed price.** Spot on the valuation date, in real terms.
2. **The central case holds it flat after inflation.** Muddle Through keeps spot constant in real
   terms, so the A$ price rises with inflation. The company is a price taker; the model takes no
   view on price without a reason.
3. **Any other path needs a stated reason.** The other scenarios move from Muddle Through only for
   a mechanism in their own narrative (real rates, inflation, official buying, supply and demand),
   written in the scenario file beside the numbers.
4. **Gold is a monetary asset.** Its real price holds with inflation and falls only for a named
   force, such as real rates well above zero or very low inflation expectations. Never because it
   looks high against history. Orderly Convergence keeps its 10% real decline on this basis.
5. **Industrial metals follow supply and demand.** For copper the current view is a world short of
   supply; a path below spot needs a reason such as demand destruction.
6. **AUD/USD is set with the commodity paths** so each scenario's story holds. Muddle Through holds
   today's rate.
7. **Outside forecasts are reference only.** Government, World Bank and bank forecasts may be shown
   beside the model, not adopted. Government and World Bank forecasts are judged low quality for
   prices: commodities are one input to their economic forecasts.
8. **Always show what the market needs.** Each miner discloses the value at spot held flat and the
   multiple of the primary commodity path that gives the market price.
9. **Same rules for every commodity** (oil, iron ore, coal).

**Consensus, when available.** When a specialist consensus forecast is available (commodity-analyst
or broker consensus), it will be shown as one of the scenarios or as a selectable option beside
them. It does not replace the scenario paths without a further decision.

**Known gap.** Costs move with CPI plus the scenario's real escalator, not with the commodity price.

### D-104 (ruled 20 Oct 2026, built the same evening). Costs follow the price

D-102 named the gap: costs moved with CPI plus the scenario escalator but not with the gold
price, so a high-gold scenario kept FY27 costs for thirty years. The evidence note
(`evn_cost_vs_gold_evidence_2026-10-20.md`, EVN FY22-FY26) put the non-royalty AISC
response at about 0.19 nominal (0.08-0.12 from FY23). The rule, as ruled:

1. **What moves.** Non-royalty cash cost and sustaining capital: in the engine, the gross
   cost (AISC with the copper credit added back and the guidance royalty taken out) and the
   sustaining capital line. Royalties already follow revenue. Mine development, project
   capital, exploration and corporate cost do not move: the first two are a mine plan, the
   last two are not in the evidence.
2. **How much.** 1.5% for each 10% real move in the primary commodity's price, linear in the
   move as the rule is stated (not a power law), so a 50% real rise adds 7.5% to the linked
   costs and a 10% fall takes 1.5% off (symmetric).
3. **Measured how.** The real price in the functional currency (the nominal A$ path over the
   scenario's CPI index, which is the USD real series over AUD/USD), against the scenario's
   own valuation-date level (period 0). Guidance costs are struck at today's price, so
   Muddle Through, flat real at spot, carries no response at all; the link only separates
   the scenarios that move away from spot. A stronger or weaker AUD counts, because the
   costs are in AUD.
4. **When.** One year late: period t reads the real price of period t-1. The stub and
   FY28 both read the valuation-date price, so the first response lands in FY29.
5. **How far.** Capped at 20% above the CPI-plus-escalator cost path. At 1.5% per 10% the cap
   binds after a real rise of about 130%; no scenario reaches it (the largest multiplier is
   Fragmentation's 1.035 in FY31).
6. **Where it lives.** `cost_price_link` on the archetype's `resource_lifecycle` block
   (`data/industries/gold_producers.yaml`): elasticity, lag, cap, symmetry and the
   sensitivity range, each with its tag. A company may override any field in
   `normalised_baseline.cost_price_link`; EVN overrides none. The engine
   (`cost_price_link_path`) takes a real price path and returns a multiplier per period;
   it names no commodity. The translator builds the path from whichever series the
   archetype declares as `primary`, so the same rule serves an oil, iron-ore or coal
   archetype unchanged.
7. **The market-implied multiple.** The gold path scaled by the multiple is read against the
   UNscaled spot, because the cost base is struck at the actual price: a path held 48% above
   spot carries the cost response to it (about +7% on linked costs from FY29). The multiple
   therefore rose from 1.44 to 1.48 when the link was built. The spot-held-flat disclosure
   is unchanged by construction.
8. **Sensitivities.** The declared range 0.5% to 3.0% per 10%, and the link switched off,
   are sized for every scenario in set `evn` (`cost_price_link_low`, `_high`, `_off`). Off
   reproduces the D-103 levels exactly, which is the reconciliation.

## 3. The mining driver set and where each driver lives

1. **Volume:** FY27 guidance by mine (layer 1, `data/financials/evn.yaml`), held to the stated
   life; project uplifts in layer 2 (`data/companies/evn.yaml`).
2. **Realised price:** scenario series (D-99) times the CPI index, over AUD/USD; less the
   stream at Northparkes.
3. **AISC and cost-curve placement:** guidance AISC by mine, with the copper credit added
   back and the royalty (3.1% of metal revenue, derived from FY26) taken out, so both move
   with their own prices. Placement is the lowest-cost quartile (`bottom_quartile`). An industry cost-curve anchor is not
   yet sourced (gap 4).
4. **Reserve life and replacement:** the inventory basis and the zero-NPV replacement rule
   (D-97, D-98), declared on the archetype's `resource_lifecycle` block.
5. **Jurisdiction:** Australia and Ontario, both rated low. No country-risk premium.
6. **Stranded-asset path:** low for gold. The transition shows up as a cost, carried by the
   Disorderly Climate real escalator.

## 4. The engine, one period at a time

For each mine and period t (period 0 is the stub, 0.693 of FY27):

1. Production = guidance rate x min(period fraction, remaining inventory / rate), for every
   metal; the mine stops when the first metal runs out.
2. Revenue = gold x AUD gold price x (1 - stream share x 0.9) + copper x AUD copper price.
3. Operating cost = (AISC x oz + copper x guidance copper price - guidance royalty -
   sustaining capital) x scale x cost index x cost-price link (D-104: 1 + 0.15 x the real
   primary-price move at t-1 from period 0, capped at 1.20). Sustaining capital inside
   capex carries the same link; mine development does not.
4. Royalty = 3.1% x revenue. Exploration and corporate (A$180/oz) are expensed.
5. Tax = 30% x (EBITDA - book D&A per ounce x ounces).
6. Capex = sustaining + mine development (per ounce, to closure) + project capital (run-off).
7. Working capital = -5% of the change in annualised revenue (FY24 and FY25 average,
   rounded per D-29), and the balance unwinds at closure.
8. FCFF is discounted at the WACC from the valuation date, mid-period.

## 6. Gaps and shortcomings found

### In the EVN data

1. The FY26 statutory accounts are not in the folder. Rehabilitation and leases are 30 June
   2025 figures. There is no FY26 revenue, D&A or tax line.
2. Cumulative stream deliveries towards the 630koz step-down are not found. The model holds
   the higher share for life, which is conservative.
3. Metallurgical recovery is found for Cowal only (85%). It is applied to every mine in the
   reserves sensitivity.
4. Northparkes E22's volume uplift is not quantified, so its capital is charged without its
   metal.

5. **Royalties are one group rate (3.1%).** The June quarterly shows royalty per sold ounce
   by mine (Cowal 213, Ernest Henry 805, Northparkes 839, Mungari 206, Red Lake nil). The
   year-1 effect of using the group rate is about A$1m.
6. **E22 may be undercharged.** The company puts it at A$545m over about five years.
   Northparkes project capital in the run-off totals about A$285m for FY27-FY30, which also
   covers the two recovery projects (about A$147m), unless the rest sits in mine
   development.
7. **The February 2026 stream amendment is not modelled.** It brings an A$120m refundable
   deposit due 15 December 2026, a lower stream rate on the E44 deposit, and a minimum
   delivery of 45koz from FY31 to FY38. E44 is outside the mine plan and the deposit is
   matched by a liability, so the value effect is nil on today's mine plan.

### In the framework, found by building the first miner

1. **The engine list was closed.** `adding_a_company.md` named three engines. A fourth needed
   a ruling, and the step should name the resource engine once D-96 is ruled.
2. **The scenarios carried no commodity prices or exchange rates.** A miner cannot be valued
   without them. They were added as typed series. The next resource company (oil, iron
   ore, coal) will need its own series with the same discipline.
3. **D-73's "proxies, not new series" rule needs a carve-out** for prices that no existing
   series can proxy. D-99 asks for it.
4. **No industry cost curve is sourced.** The archetype says where it would go. An average
   and a 90th-percentile AISC from a primary source (World Gold Council or S&P) are needed
   before the cost-curve claim is more than a qualitative statement.
5. **The SSOT valuation-date check assumed a 30 September year-end.** It now reads the
   year-end from layer 1. CSL (June year-end) has never declared a valuation date, so
   nothing had tested it.
6. **The moat-role test treated "all rent" as an error.** For a miner it is the intended
   reading; it is now a declared field (`no_barrier_declared`), still visible.
7. **The D-62 disclosures do not translate cleanly.** TV / capital and implied moat assume a
   persistent capital base. For a depleting asset the honest disclosures are the run-off
   share, the life, and the value per share on each inventory basis. The seven-disclosure
   list in `adding_a_company.md` should say so.
8. **The risk-free rate is now on two dates** (4.30-4.50% for the June companies, 5.31% for
   EVN). The cross-company comparison in the UI should say so until one date is set.
9. **The UI, workbook and session-start base tie do not include EVN yet.** The generator is
   built around the three existing engines (D-23: no JS port, so the page must read engine
   output). This is its own sitting. *UI and base tie built 20 Oct 2026 evening
   (`ui_prototypes/_generator/resource_cfg.py`, `evn_scenario_interface.html`,
   `tests/test_ui_evn_gap9.py`); the page carries the gap 7 disclosures and the gap 8 note.
   The formula workbook is still open.*
10. **The register lint treats every number in a guidance table as a register value.** It
    flags coincidences with UI pixel widths. Sixteen were baselined. A guidance table is
    observed data and might be better exempt by path.

## 8. Stephen's review, 20 October 2026 afternoon

1. **Gold.** Stephen rejected paths that fall below spot. Gold is a financial asset; inflation
   has already devalued the major currencies against it, and that is not reversed without very
   low inflation expectations, which are unlikely in the current political setting. Inflation
   in AUD revenue and costs goes with a gold price that holds. Producers often model flat real
   prices. The paths were re-set (§2, D-99): Muddle Through flat real at spot, the others as
   stated moves from it. Open: whether Orderly Convergence should also be flat (A$5.73) rather
   than 10% below (A$4.88).
2. **Copper method.** The first draft was typed judgement from each scenario's metals narrative,
   starting from EVN's September quarter achieved price, with no anchor. Revised to the gold
   discipline: Muddle Through flat real at US$14,750, the others as stated moves, on Stephen's
   view that the world is short copper in every scenario. Only Stagflation dips below spot.
   Copper matters less than gold: on Muddle Through, 10% off copper costs about A$0.52 a share,
   against A$1.26 for 10% off gold. (Company sensitivity, FY27: A$100/oz gold or A$1,000/t copper
   each move cash flow A$65-70m.)
3. **AUD/USD method.** Typed judgement from each scenario's currency narrative, starting from the
   19 October close (0.6968): Muddle Through flat, the others move with their USD story. No
   interest-rate-parity or purchasing-power model. A 10% stronger AUD costs about A$1.62 a share
   on Muddle Through, more than 10% off gold, because costs are in AUD.
4. **Beta.** 0.90 is judgement from five named peers whose betas are not observed in the repo
   (D-02 status). One published measure puts EVN's beta at 1.62 (Simply Wall St; window and
   index not stated). Stephen suspects it could be high. On the revised paths: beta 1.2 gives a
   WACC of 10.98% and Muddle Through A$5.90; beta 1.6 gives 12.87% and A$5.25. Observed betas for
   EVN and the peers are needed before D-101 is ruled.
5. **Terminal value (presentation ruled: "life-of-mine cash flows after year 10").** The model already runs every mine to the end of its stated life, mine by
   mine, to FY54. There is no perpetuity and no growth rate. The "terminal" is only the present
   value of years 11 to closure, kept separate so the ten-year split of D-71 can be shown. For a
   miner it would read better labelled "life-of-mine cash flows after year 10" (gap 7).
