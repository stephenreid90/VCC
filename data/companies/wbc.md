# Westpac Banking Corporation (ASX:WBC) — company position

Westpac Banking Corporation is the second-largest of the Big Four by total assets and the #2 mortgage book holder in the Australian system. Anchor period for this position is the half-year ended 31 March 2026 (1H26), sourced from the 1H26 financial results announcement, 1H26 investor discussion pack, March 2026 Pillar 3 report, and the FY25 annual report. Position decomposed force-by-force against the Australian major banks industry archetype.

## Structural position

WBC's franchise is anchored on the consumer-banking and mortgage core: AUD 517.7bn Australian housing book at 31 March 2026, AUD 18.5bn RAMS book (now in run-off following the strategic decision to discontinue new RAMS originations), AUD 9.1bn personal lending, plus the deposit franchise. Business banking is the rebuild project — AUD 238bn Australian business loans, currently #3 in the market behind NAB (#1) and CBA (#2 after a multi-year rebuild). Institutional banking via WIB sits as the third pillar at ~17% of revenue with stable contribution. Westpac New Zealand contributes ~12% of revenue at the AUD-translated level.

The interim result for 1H26 shows the post-Miller franchise stabilisation in numbers: cash NPAT ex notable items AUD 3,483m (+1% half-on-half, +1% versus 1H25), revenue AUD 11,284m (+3% versus 1H25), expense growth held to +2% versus 1H25 even while transformation investment continues. NIM ex notable 1.89% (core 1.78% + Treasury & Markets 0.11bps), having compressed 4bps on core versus 2H25 — the front-book mortgage competition is moderating but has not reversed. Impairment charges AUD 443m represents an annualised loss rate of ~10bps of average loans, still benign-cycle but normalising from the post-COVID write-back-supported low.

Capital position is comfortably above target: Level 2 CET1 12.42%, against management's 11.0-11.5% operating range, leaving ~92bps of buffer above the upper end of target. On-market buyback continuing (AUD 1.5bn announced FY24; AUD 581m executed FY25; balance progressing through FY26). AT1 capital outstanding AUD 8.5bn.

## Five Forces — WBC position vs the Big Four average

**Buyer power**. Industry rating LOW-to-MODERATE. WBC sits at industry average. Retail customer behaviour is the same as the Big Four norm — sticky back-book, rate-sensitive front-book — and the business banking customer mix is slightly more SME-weighted than NAB. No material company-specific differentiation. Impact: 0.

**Supplier power**. Industry rating MODERATE. WBC sits LOWER (favourable) on the funding-cost dimension. The retail deposit franchise is structural — #2 Australian household deposit share, AUD 615bn Australian retail deposit base including AUD 149bn non-interest-bearing transaction balances. The non-interest-bearing transaction deposit pool is the lever: it provides a structural funding-cost advantage versus more wholesale-funded peers (ANZ being the most wholesale-weighted). The advantage is real on a 5-10 year view — equivalent to roughly 5-10bps NIM benefit captured in the through-cycle NIM. Impact: +8bps on NIM.

**Threat of new entrants**. Industry rating LOW. WBC at industry average — same D-SIB-protected position as the rest of the Big Four. The +1.0% D-SIB surcharge is uniform across the Big Four; WBC has no franchise-specific protection beyond the archetype-level moat. Impact: 0.

**Threat of substitutes**. Industry rating LOW-to-MODERATE. WBC at industry average. Non-bank mortgage share-loss exposure is proportional to mortgage book scale — at #2 the absolute dollar exposure is meaningful but the relative loss rate is the same as the Big Four. Westpac NZ exposure to NZ non-bank lenders is similar. Impact: 0.

**Rivalry**. Industry rating MODERATE. WBC sits HIGHER (unfavourable). The binding evidence is cost-to-income: WBC at 51.7% versus CBA at 42% and NAB at 43%. Translates to roughly 150bps of ROE drag versus the leaders that the transformation program (UNITE, multi-year cost-out plan FY26-FY28) is intended to close. The framework treats most of this as a transformation overlay (margin glide via §3.2) rather than a structural offset, because the cost-out path is management-committed and tracking. The residual structural drag (~10bps revenue growth) reflects the share-loss vulnerability that comes with running at higher cost-to-income.

A sub-component of rivalry: business-banking rebuild. WBC lost meaningful share to NAB and CBA over 2018-2022. Re-investment in business bankers and digital tooling from FY24 onward has stabilised share but not yet meaningfully recovered it. A further ~5bps revenue growth drag specifically on the business segment until the rebuild closes (estimated FY28).

**Net company-position offset**. The structural offsets sum to:

| Component | Force | Direction | Quantified |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Funding-cost advantage from retail deposit franchise | Supplier power | Favourable | +8bps NIM |
| Cost-to-income gap to peer leaders | Rivalry | Unfavourable | −10bps revenue growth |
| Business banking rebuild | Rivalry (sub) | Unfavourable | −5bps growth (3-5 yr) |
| Buyer / new entrants / substitutes | (others) | Neutral | 0 |

Net: NIM +8bps offset; revenue growth −5bps net; transformation overlay closes 150bps of cost-to-income gap on a glide path. The transformation overlay is the dominant forward-looking lever.

## What the framework will do

The Westpac valuation under §15 will:

1. Forecast NII from average interest-earning assets growth × NIM trajectory (scenario-conditional on cash-rate path and competitive intensity).
2. Forecast non-interest income separately (fee income, markets income, treasury) under scenario macro inputs.
3. Forecast operating expenses with the transformation glide (cost-to-income drift from 51.7% toward ~45% over FY26-FY29).
4. Forecast credit losses as a primary scenario driver — through-cycle anchor 18bps, scenario-conditional on the macro_baseline.credit_cycle block.
5. Forecast CET1 generation and check it against the dividend payout and asset-growth constraint.
6. Discount at constant cost of equity (single-Ke discipline per §15.2(c)) — expected range 8.55-9.30% per the archetype anchor; bank-specific to be calibrated.
7. Compute terminal value via the Gordon-growth-on-equity formulation (§15.8) with terminal ROE in the 10-12% range.
8. Bridge to per-share equity value with the §15.7 deductions (AT1 hybrid AUD 8.5bn outstanding) and divide by share count.

The Muddle Through scenario is the first build target; the other five scenarios extend per the impact matrix in the next step.
