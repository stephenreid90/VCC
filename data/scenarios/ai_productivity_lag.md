# AI Productivity Lag

**Scenario ID:** `ai_productivity_lag`
**Version:** 2026-Q2-v1
**Published:** 16 May 2026
**Role in scenario set:** Technology / productivity boundary.

---

## The scenario in one paragraph

The Solow productivity paradox plays out at AI scale. AI capability advances genuinely — foundation models keep improving, agents proliferate, infrastructure spend stays high (deficit-funded, US-led). The bull case for economy-wide productivity transformation, though, doesn't materialise on the bull's timeline. Enterprise adoption is slow: workflow integration is hard; trust and audit requirements slow rollout in regulated industries; behavioural change in workforce takes years not quarters; most firms run with status quo plus light AI augmentation rather than transformation. Productivity gains concentrate in well-integrated firms within sectors most amenable to AI substitution; aggregate TFP grows ~0.5% — modestly above pre-pandemic norm but well short of the 1.5%+ that current investment levels imply. Rents capture is asymmetric: platform owners (model providers, compute, cloud) capture the bulk of AI rents via subscriptions; broader enterprise pays for AI tools without proportionate productivity payback. Equity-market concentration in platform owners intensifies. The saving grace is the labour-cost channel: AI does materially contain wage growth in white-collar / professional / clerical roles and addresses labour shortages — a different transmission from productivity, but real. Margin-positive for labour-intensive corporates even where productivity transmission is slow. China's bipedal-robotics-plus-AI approach has tangible deployment in physical-substitution use cases (factories), giving it relative advantage. Inflation runs slightly above Muddle Through; real rates stay higher than the bull case implies; growth modestly below; sectoral dispersion wide between platform winners and broad-enterprise losers.

## Macro picture

Global growth at 1.9–2.0% — modestly below Muddle Through reflecting the productivity surprise failing to materialise. Headline inflation in advanced economies around 3.0% — similar to Muddle Through. Real policy rates 1.0%, similar to Muddle Through. Aggregate TFP growth in advanced economies around 0.5% — modestly above pre-pandemic but well below bull-case expectations. The key macro point: the scenario looks like Muddle Through on headline numbers but the *story* underneath is fundamentally different.

## US powerhouse stance

The US powerhouse persists structurally but the AI-driven productivity differential that bulls expect to compound US outperformance doesn't materialise. US firms (and especially US platform owners) capture the rents from the AI investment cycle — those firms compound — but the broader US economy doesn't get the lift. The US still leads but the lift is concentrated in a narrow cohort.

## Climate policy posture

Continues to drift as in Muddle Through. No crystallisation event; no political reset. AI-energy demand (data centre power consumption) becomes a climate-policy flashpoint as renewable build-out struggles to keep pace; emissions trajectory of the AI infrastructure footprint itself becomes politically visible.

## AI and productivity — the central story

**Capability and capex continue at high levels.** Foundation models advance; agents proliferate; AI infrastructure spend stays high. No financial-market unwind. The investment side of the bull case delivers.

**Enterprise adoption is slow.** Workflow integration is harder than the bull case assumed; trust and audit requirements slow rollout in regulated industries (finance, healthcare); behavioural change in workforce is slow. Most firms run with status quo plus light AI augmentation rather than transformation. Penetration plateaus outside software / professional services / content sectors.

**Productivity transmission is narrow.** Gains concentrated in well-integrated firms within sectors most amenable to AI substitution. Economy-wide TFP grows ~0.5% — modestly above pre-pandemic norm but well short of the bull-case 1.5%+ that justifies current AI investment levels.

**Rents capture is asymmetric, platform-favoured.** Big tech (model providers, compute / cloud, platform owners) captures the bulk of AI rents via subscriptions, compute, model access. Most enterprises pay for AI tools but don't extract proportional economic value. Equity-market concentration in platform owners intensifies. Reminiscent of how electrical-utility diffusion played out (infrastructure providers benefited before downstream users did).

**Labour-cost channel works.** AI materially contains wage growth in white-collar / professional / clerical roles and addresses labour shortages. Different transmission from productivity: wages stay contained even where TFP transmission is slow. Margin-positive for labour-intensive corporates that adopt AI for cost-containment even where they don't realise productivity gains.

## Geopolitics and trade

Bloc-fragmentation themes from Fragmentation partly apply (AI stacks separating) but at lower intensity; trade frictions slow rather than accelerate. AI hardware export controls continue; broader trade settings stable. Critical-mineral access for AI hardware (rare earths, gallium, germanium) modestly contested; semiconductor supply chains under ongoing strategic scrutiny.

## Demographic response

The cross-cutting insight that emerged from the workshop: **AI doesn't need to deliver economy-wide TFP gains to be useful for labour substitution.** China's bipedal-robotics-plus-AI approach has tangible deployment in physical-substitution use cases (factories) even though general AI penetration is slow. The labour-cost containment channel works in cognitive-substitution domains (white-collar work) too, even where productivity transmission doesn't.

Australia / Europe — high-skill-labour-intensive, no robotics base, immigration-constrained — feel the demographic squeeze most acutely. The combination of slow AI penetration + tapering immigration + no robotics base creates a triple constraint on labour availability.

## Sectoral picture

Wide dispersion. **Platform owners** (Big tech, AI infrastructure providers, compute / cloud) compound; equity-market concentration intensifies. **Labour-intensive incumbents** with high-skill workforces (financial services, healthcare, professional services) benefit from the labour-cost containment channel. **Broad enterprise** without AI integration capability under-performs. **Growth equities** for AI beneficiaries see multiples gradually compress as the productivity-surprise narrative fades, even as earnings hold up. **Defensives** broadly Muddle-Through-like.

## How the world becomes AI Productivity Lag

No trigger event required — this scenario is the default if the bull case for AI fails to deliver economy-wide. The conditions are largely in place: enterprise-adoption surveys already show penetration concentrated in narrow cohorts; productivity transmission to aggregate TFP is currently subdued despite high investment; platform-owner rent capture is visible in equity-market concentration. The scenario describes the world that emerges if these patterns extend through the horizon rather than breaking out.

## What would invalidate this scenario

Sustained enterprise-AI adoption broadening with productivity flow-through to aggregate TFP (would shift toward Orderly Convergence). AI capex collapse or financial-market unwind (would shift toward a different scenario closer to original "Tech-Investment Unwind" framing, not in current set — could become a stress-test variant). Major acute non-AI shock (would shift toward Stagflation Persists, Fragmentation, or Disorderly Climate Crystallisation depending on the shock).

## Implications for IPL, CSL, WBC (brief)

**IPL** — pays for AI tools but doesn't extract much rent; mining-customer productivity gains slow; modest labour-cost containment in own operations. Net: small headwind on demand-side, small margin-helper from labour costs. Roughly neutral.

**CSL** — high-skill labour-intensive; meaningful labour-cost containment via AI augmentation; AI-assisted drug discovery is one of the higher-return areas for AI rent extraction, so CSL is better-positioned than the typical enterprise. Pipeline-NPV multiples don't expand as fast as bull case but the labour-cost story is supportive. Net: small positive on costs, mid-case on multiples, neutral-to-slightly-positive overall.

**WBC** — high-skill labour-intensive (analysts, advisers, compliance, technology); meaningful labour-cost containment / cost-to-income improvement but slower than bulls hope; modest tech-lending book observation but no credit event. Net: positive on cost line, neutral elsewhere. WBC is one of the better-positioned of our three on this scenario.
