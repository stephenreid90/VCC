# Disorderly Climate Crystallisation

**Scenario ID:** `disorderly_climate_crystallisation`
**Version:** 2026-Q2-v1
**Published:** 16 May 2026
**Role in scenario set:** Climate boundary. Specifically about the crystallisation event — *not* about whether climate transition becomes disorderly (it already is, per the workshop's framing shift).

---

## The scenario in one paragraph

The disorderly climate path that's already in motion has its crystallisation moment. Trigger arrives — most likely a confluence rather than a single event: a major physical-loss event (catastrophic extreme weather hitting an insured population centre), forced regulator-led de-leveraging of carbon-intensive bank loan books, a sudden carbon-price spike in a major jurisdiction. Policy snaps from drift to abrupt action under crisis conditions: carbon prices spike to EUR 200+ in advanced economies; carbon-border-adjustment mechanisms force pricing onto imports; subsidies for renewables and EVs are massively expanded; emergency funding for climate-adaptation infrastructure unlocks. Capital reallocates abruptly — flight from fossil fuels, energy-intensive manufacturing, traditional auto, exposed real estate; flood into renewables, EVs, transition minerals, adaptation infrastructure. Asset-price dispersion widens sharply; some sectors realise large stranded-asset losses, others see multiple-expansion windfalls. Inflation runs persistently 3–5% as carbon costs flow through supply chains; central banks face the policy dilemma between supply-shock tightening and reallocation accommodation, resulting in choppy rates with elevated volatility. Insurance withdraws from exposed regions; bank loan books in carbon-intensive sectors take write-downs. Trade frictions intensify around carbon-border adjustment and transition-mineral supply. Aggregate growth near Muddle Through but with much wider sectoral dispersion: transition winners boom, losers face stranded-asset realisation. The scenario that tests terminal-state convergence discipline hardest — many assumed-perpetual moats turn out to have stranded-asset half-lives that crystallise inside the forecast horizon.

## Macro picture

Global growth at 2.0–2.2% — aggregate near Muddle Through, but the sectoral dispersion is much wider. Headline inflation in advanced economies peaks at 4.5–5% in year 3 from carbon-cost pass-through, then settles around 4% before easing to 3% by year 10. Real policy rates 1.5%, but with elevated volatility through the repricing phase. EU ETS carbon price spikes to EUR 200+ in year 2 from triggers; settles around EUR 200–220 in the new equilibrium. Insurance-sector pricing materially repriced upward in physical-risk-exposed regions.

## US powerhouse stance

The US could be either the resilient outperformer (if it leans into transition as economic opportunity, capturing transition-mineral / clean-tech leadership) or the laggard (if political backlash dominates and the response is reactive rather than strategic). Most plausibly bifurcates by state — California / Northeast lead, Texas / Florida lag. Federal posture oscillates. Aggregate US position depends on which faction dominates the response.

## Climate policy posture

Accelerated and uneven. EU and other advanced jurisdictions impose carbon prices in the EUR 200+ range — a step-change from current EUR 60–80. Carbon-border-adjustment mechanisms force pricing onto imports. Emerging markets stay laggard on direct carbon pricing but get caught by border-adjustment. Renewable / EV / adaptation subsidies massively expanded. Climate becomes the dominant organising principle of regulation through the crystallisation phase.

## AI and productivity

Continues at Muddle-Through-like pace. AI data-centre power demand becomes a climate-policy flashpoint (significant electricity / cooling consumption clashes with renewable-build constraints). AI plays role in climate-adaptation planning, transition logistics, and physical-risk modelling. Not the dominant story.

## Geopolitics and trade

Climate becomes a geopolitical organising principle alongside bloc dynamics. Carbon-border-adjustment mechanisms create trade friction; climate-financing disputes intensify between advanced and developing economies; transition-mineral supply becomes acute strategic priority. The bloc-fragmentation themes from Fragmentation partly apply but the dominant frame is climate-driven realignment rather than security-driven.

## Demographic response

Climate-driven migration becomes a significant complicating factor. Climate-displaced labour is large but unskilled, doesn't fill skilled-migration gaps, and is politically toxic in destination countries. Compounded with declining skilled-migration pool (the underlying demographic momentum from all scenarios), this is the worst labour-availability scenario. Eldercare specifically becomes a binding constraint everywhere. China's robotics-response continues to be relatively well-positioned; Western advanced economies struggle most with the climate-plus-demographic dual squeeze.

## Sectoral picture — the dispersion story

Sharp bifurcation between transition winners and stranded losers.

**Transition winners** — renewables, storage, EVs, hydrogen, CCS, climate-adaptation infrastructure, transition minerals (lithium, copper, nickel, rare earths), grid investment, energy efficiency. Capital floods in, multiples expand, growth accelerates.

**Stranded losers** — thermal coal mining (acutely), oil-sands services, energy-intensive heavy industry (cement, steel, aluminium without low-carbon credentials), traditional ICE auto, fossil-fuel-dependent emerging-market sovereigns. Stranded-asset write-downs realise. Multiple compression severe.

**Caught in between** — utilities (depends on generation mix), insurance (revenue support but physical-risk losses), banks (loan-book stress in carbon-intensive exposures offset by transition-financing opportunities), real estate (location-specific physical-risk repricing).

## How the world becomes Disorderly Climate Crystallisation

Trigger is most likely a confluence rather than a single event. Plausible catalysts include: a major US hurricane or wildfire causing insured losses above USD 200bn; APRA or ECB or Fed climate stress tests forcing visible bank-capital reallocations; a sudden EU ETS price move to EUR 150+ unlocking carbon-border-adjustment expansion; a stranded-asset write-down at a major energy or mining company crystallising sector-wide repricing; emergence of insurance-sector withdrawal from key regions becoming politically visible. Once two or three of these converge, the policy snap from drift to action becomes self-reinforcing.

## What would invalidate this scenario

Continued drift without crystallisation (would shift toward Muddle Through). Major political reset enabling orderly transition (would shift toward Orderly Convergence). Climate-policy backsliding under macro crisis (would shift toward Stagflation's climate-stagnant posture).

## Implications for IPL, CSL, WBC (brief)

**IPL** — significant transition exposure. Mining-customer base includes substantial fossil-fuel mining (thermal coal, oil-sands services); transition-mineral mining (lithium, copper, nickel, rare earths) accelerates and is positive for IPL. Net depends on customer-mix shift speed. Own emissions exposure (ammonia production is carbon-intensive) means carbon-pricing flow-through to margins. Net: negative on fossil-fuel-exposed business segments; positive on transition-mineral demand; multiple-compression as ESG re-rating happens.

**CSL** — relatively insulated. Specialty biologics not directly climate-exposed; supply chain has some carbon-cost flow-through; potential growth in climate-adapted-healthcare segments (heat-related illness, infectious-disease spread). Net: small negative on costs, small positive on demand mix; multiples broadly preserved given limited transition exposure.

**WBC** — materially exposed via loan book. Stranded-asset write-downs in fossil-fuel-exposed commercial lending; physical-risk losses on mortgages in climate-exposed regions (parts of NSW / QLD coast, Murray-Darling agriculture); insurance-sector linkages where WBC is exposed to insurer counterparty risk; offsetting positive exposure to transition-financing opportunities. APRA prudential focus on climate risk forces capital reallocation. Net: significant credit-quality stress in carbon-intensive parts of the book; offsetting growth opportunities in transition financing; overall negative in the early years, potentially positive longer-run.
