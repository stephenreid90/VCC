# Fragmentation and Resource Nationalism

**Scenario ID:** `fragmentation`
**Version:** 2026-Q2-v1
**Published:** 16 May 2026
**Role in scenario set:** Geopolitical / trade boundary.

---

## The scenario in one paragraph

The Russia-NATO standoff doesn't resolve and the US-China contest hardens. Trade-as-weapon becomes routine — tariffs, sanctions, export controls extend beyond critical technology into broader industrial and commodity flows. Supply chains regionalise: the US, EU, and allies build redundant industrial capacity within friendly jurisdictions; China builds the parallel system its scale enables. Energy markets bifurcate, with Russian, Iranian, and Venezuelan crude flowing to China-aligned destinations at structural discount; US shale and Gulf supply the rest. Critical minerals access becomes the new geopolitical front — transition minerals, semiconductors, biotech inputs, AI hardware. The duplicated industrial footprint costs money: inflation runs persistently in the 3–4% range, real rates higher than Muddle Through. AI stacks separate: US/EU and Chinese ecosystems develop in parallel; Chinese capability matures faster than Western consensus credits and dominates within its bloc. Growth bifurcates — China-aligned economies (China itself, Africa where engagement deepens, parts of LatAm) may outpace the US-aligned bloc, which pays the cost of supply-chain duplication and constrained migrant access. Europe is hit hardest. Australia faces its strategic alignment question acutely — security with the US-aligned bloc, but current dependency on China-supplied automation and on Chinese demand for its exports. The new equilibrium is structurally inferior to the pre-fragmentation world.

## Macro picture

Global growth at 2.0% — modestly below Muddle Through reflecting supply-chain duplication costs. Bifurcated by bloc: China-aligned economies at 3.5–4.0%, US-aligned at 1.8%. Inflation in advanced economies 3.5%, higher than Muddle Through. Real rates 1.5%. USD share of reserves declines gradually but remains dominant; CNY gains share within China-aligned bloc; gold prominent as cross-bloc settlement medium.

## US powerhouse stance

The US sustains within its bloc but faces capacity / cost-of-supply-chain-duplication friction. Relative outperformance persists but at higher cost than Muddle Through. The US is the leader of its bloc; Chinese bloc grows faster than US-aligned bloc through the structural-adjustment phase. The US powerhouse becomes a bloc-specific story rather than a global-engine story.

## Climate policy posture

Bloc-divergent. EU maintains aggressive carbon-pricing within bloc; US under continued political contestation; China-aligned bloc pursues transition for strategic / industrial-policy reasons rather than climate reasons. Cross-bloc carbon-border-adjustment mechanisms become trade-war instruments rather than climate policy. Aggregate emissions trajectory remains poor as cross-bloc coordination collapses.

## AI and productivity

Bifurcates. Within blocs, productivity continues — but cross-bloc technology transfer constrained. Chinese AI matures faster than Western consensus credits; parallel ecosystems develop with their own model architectures, training data regimes, deployment patterns. AI hardware supply chains separate. China's strategic position in physical AI (bipedal robots, autonomous systems for factories and logistics) gives it material advantage on demographic-adaptation grounds — see below.

## Geopolitics and trade

Formal or de facto bloc formation: US-aligned (NATO + AUKUS + Quad + East Asia treaty network), China-aligned (BRICS+, SCO, Belt-and-Road partners), non-aligned (India, Brazil, ASEAN core, parts of Africa) navigating carefully but increasingly forced to choose. Bloc-internal trade grows; bloc-crossing trade contracts. Tariffs widen across goods categories; export-control regimes tighten in dual-use, critical minerals, biotech, AI hardware. Cross-border M&A increasingly blocked on security grounds. Critical-minerals access weaponised. Energy-supply contracts bloc-aligned. Transition-mineral cooperation only within bloc.

## Demographic response

**Most differentiated scenario.** China's response is uniquely decisive because it's self-sufficient — industrial base + AI + robotics + state-directed capital — and doesn't depend on a contracting global migrant pool. The strategic value of China's robotics capability for the rest of the world rises sharply; countries without domestic robotics manufacturing become dependent on buying robots from China and Japan / Germany. Under Fragmentation, the US-aligned bloc loses (or pays a premium for) Chinese-supplied automation — compounding the demographic-adaptation problem.

Australia faces an acute strategic question. Its automation needs run primarily through China-supplied imports today; its security alignment is with the US-aligned bloc. The geopolitical alignment Australia chooses materially shapes its demographic-adaptation cost. African migration corridors become strategic instruments — China-Africa engagement positions China to access African demographic dividend just as Western migration corridors face political friction.

## Sectoral picture

Wide sector dispersion. Bloc-internal manufacturers benefit from onshoring capex. Strategic resource exporters (critical minerals, rare earths, food staples) outperform. Multinationals with significant cross-bloc operations re-rate lower. Bloc-internal services and infrastructure sustain growth. Energy companies bifurcate by bloc — Russian / Iranian / Venezuelan supply discounted to China-aligned; US / Gulf supply at premium to US-aligned bloc.

## How the world becomes Fragmentation

Triggers are likely incremental rather than singular: a sequence of sanctions packages, export-control announcements, alliance-formation moments, and reserve-diversification evidence that cumulatively cross a threshold. Major catalysts could include Russia-Ukraine settlement failing, US-China trade-framework negotiations collapsing, sanctions escalations against Iran or China, a major Taiwan-related incident.

## What would invalidate this scenario

Major diplomatic breakthroughs (US-China summit yielding substantive trade framework, Russia-Ukraine settlement, Iran nuclear deal revival) would weaken the bloc-formation logic. Sustained globalisation gains in lower-sensitivity categories would suggest barrier-stability rather than ratcheting.

## Implications for DNL, CSL, WBC (brief)

**DNL** — most-exposed of our three. Industrial explosives traded globally; ammonia and gas inputs traded globally; supply-chain regionalisation forces duplicated production or accepts disruption. Strategic-mineral mining (Australia's bloc-strategic minerals) might lift volumes long-run, but disruption in the transition is sharp. Margin compression from supply-chain duplication costs. Net: materially negative for cyclical DNL.

**CSL** — moderately exposed. Plasma collection is geographic (US dominant); fractionation / manufacturing has some geographic flex. Strategic-biotech designations may constrain cross-bloc movement of plasma products. China's market for CSL products contracts under fragmentation. Net: modest revenue headwind; multiples compress as the global-quality-growth thesis erodes.

**WBC** — mixed. Bank-archetype credit-quality stress in the Australian SME book where supply-chain regionalisation hurts (immigration-dependent sectors particularly). Australian-dollar currency volatility if Australia's bloc-alignment becomes contested. Sovereign-aligned regulatory regime works in WBC's favour (Australian banks protected from cross-bloc competition under fragmentation). NIM benefits from structurally higher rates. Net: protective structural position, but credit-quality stress in the SME book pulls earnings down.
