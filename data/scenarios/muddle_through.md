# Muddle Through

**Scenario ID:** `muddle_through`
**Version:** 2026-Q2-v1
**Published:** 16 May 2026
**Role in scenario set:** Interior, most-likely case.

---

## The scenario in one paragraph

Post-2022 friction proves persistent rather than transitional. Inflation cools but lands above target — 2.5–3.5% in advanced economies — and central banks come to accept the structural overshoot rather than crush demand to force the last point down. Real rates settle structurally higher than the 2010s; debt-service costs constrain fiscal manoeuvring, but the system absorbs it without crisis. The US continues to outperform — fiscal expansion plus AI investment plus immigration plus structural advantages keep it the global growth engine — but the lift doesn't propagate to Europe or much of EM. China grinds through its property workout, accelerates its industrial-robotics deployment, and maintains export competitiveness through productivity rather than wage advantage. Climate policy stays patchy: regional divergence widens, periodic backsliding under cost-of-living pressure offsets renewable-build progress, the aggregate emissions trajectory drifts. AI investment continues high but productivity transmission is slow and rents concentrate in platform owners; labour-cost containment in white-collar work is the main flow-through. Geopolitical contestation continues without escalation; trade barriers stable but not relaxing. The world is recognisable, just structurally harder than the pre-pandemic norm. No crisis; no resolution; a grinding extension of present conditions.

## Macro picture

Global growth at 2.3% holds through the horizon — modestly below the pre-pandemic norm but stable. US growth around 2.4% leads; advanced-economy aggregate around 1.5–2.0%; emerging markets bifurcate by commodity exposure and structural reform progress. Headline inflation in advanced economies settles at 3.0% — above the 2% targets central banks notionally hold, but a level they tacitly accept rather than fight. Real policy rates around 1.0%, materially above the 2010s zero-bound era. The US dollar is broadly firm but range-bound; emerging-market currencies under gradual pressure from higher US real rates.

## US powerhouse stance

The US continues to outperform structurally. Fiscal expansion (USD 2tn deficits sustained), AI investment concentrated in US firms, immigration where politically tolerated, deep capital markets, and demographic resilience relative to Europe and developed Asia keep the US the world's growth engine. The outperformance is real but **doesn't propagate** — Europe stays stagnant; China continues its property workout; EM growth is uneven. The bifurcation is structural rather than cyclical.

## Climate policy posture

Patchy progress with regional divergence and periodic backsliding. The EU maintains its carbon-pricing trajectory and renewable build-out. The US continues under political contestation with periodic policy reversals around each election cycle — the Inflation Reduction Act survives but parts of it get rolled back; California continues to lead; federal climate policy oscillates. Developing-economy progress is contingent on cost-of-living and fiscal conditions. The aggregate emissions trajectory drifts modestly above stated pathways. No crystallisation event; no policy reset. The disorderly climate path that's already running continues at its current pace.

## AI and productivity

Investment continues at high levels — deficit-funded, US-led, capital available. Capability advances. But enterprise adoption is slow: workflow integration is hard, trust and audit requirements constrain rollout in regulated industries, behavioural change takes years. Productivity gains concentrate in well-integrated firms within sectors most amenable to AI substitution — software, professional services, content. Aggregate TFP grows 0.7–0.9%, modestly above the pre-pandemic norm but well short of the bull-case 1.5%+. Rents capture is asymmetric — platform owners (model providers, compute, cloud) capture the bulk; broader enterprise pays for AI tools without proportional productivity payback. Equity-market concentration in platform owners intensifies but not to bubble extremes. The labour-cost containment channel works: AI does materially contain wage growth in white-collar / professional / clerical roles. Different transmission from productivity, but real.

## Geopolitics and trade

US-China contestation continues without escalation. Sanctions architecture is intact and modestly extending — particularly in semiconductors and AI hardware — but no major new fronts open. Middle East flashpoints continue at current intensity without resolving or igniting. Russia-NATO standoff continues. Taiwan tensions managed. Trade barriers are stable but not relaxing; tariffs at post-2022 levels persist; supply chain regionalisation is gradual rather than acute. Selective resource nationalism (China rare earths, Indonesian nickel) continues. The world the institutions are publishing as their reference forecast.

## Demographic response

Each country adapts in its own way at its own pace. China grinds forward with robotics deployment — cost of capital is workable, strategic imperative is real, industrial base is in place. The US relies on immigration where politically tolerated plus AI cognitive substitution; the powerhouse persists partly via these channels. Europe falls behind on both fronts — aging acute, immigration politically contested, no domestic robotics base. Australia depends primarily on immigration and faces wage pressure when immigration becomes politically constrained. The 2040s skilled-migration cliff is beyond this scenario's explicit horizon but its shadow informs terminal-growth thinking (per architecture spec §9.7 item 9): countries with self-sufficient response capability sustain higher terminal growth; countries renting demographic time carry lower terminal assumptions.

## Sectoral picture

Cyclical industrials continue at trend; energy investment cycle in modest swing without inflection; financials benefit from structurally higher rates without recession-driven credit stress; growth equities hold a premium but multiples don't expand further; defensive sectors trade near long-term averages.

## How the world becomes Muddle Through

The path from here is the default — no single trigger event required. The conditions that would shift the world to another scenario (sustained inflation re-anchoring; political reset enabling orderly climate transition; geopolitical escalation; major financial-condition shock; AI productivity surprise) each individually fail to materialise. The cumulative absence of either a resolution shock or a crisis shock leaves the world muddling through.

## What would invalidate this scenario

Sustained inflation re-anchoring at target with credible central-bank declaration would shift toward Orderly Convergence. Inflation-expectations break above 3% with second-round wage dynamics would shift toward Stagflation Persists. A trigger event in energy, financial markets, climate, or geopolitics that forces regime change in one of those dimensions would shift toward one of the boundary scenarios.

## Implications for IPL, CSL, WBC (brief)

**IPL** — mining capex cycle continues; long-term gas contracts protect input costs; modest demand growth; mining customers absorb climate-policy costs gradually. Mid-case for IPL.

**CSL** — healthcare spend stays steady; plasma supply chain unstressed; pipeline-NPV multiples don't expand but hold; labour-cost containment from AI partly offsets Australian operations cost pressure. Mid-case for CSL.

**WBC** — credit losses normalise; deposit franchise stable; NIM normalises in the higher-real-rates regime (modest tailwind to bank profitability); cost-to-income slowly improves via AI augmentation. Arguably the most-favoured of our three because the higher-real-rates regime is structurally helpful.
