# Stagflation Persists

**Scenario ID:** `stagflation_persists`
**Version:** 2026-Q2-v1
**Published:** 16 May 2026
**Role in scenario set:** Macro-downside boundary.

---

## The scenario in one paragraph

The IMF's April 2026 severe scenario plays out. The Middle East situation doesn't fade — Strait of Hormuz disruption deepens, energy infrastructure damage compounds, energy prices spike and stay elevated. Inflation expectations break above the 3% credibility threshold in advanced economies and stay there; second-round wage-price dynamics take hold in services and labour-intensive sectors. Central banks lose the credibility they spent the post-COVID cycle rebuilding and are forced to maintain restrictive policy through the horizon. Real rates settle 2–3% — well above the post-GFC norm and well above Muddle Through. Advanced-economy growth slows materially with 1–2 years of recession inside the scenario horizon. Climate policy stagnates further as cost-of-living crisis crushes the political coalition needed for transition; carbon pricing rollbacks accelerate; emissions trajectory drifts upward. AI investment continues — deficit funding doesn't stop — but financing constraints compress the marginal capex and productivity dispersion intensifies. Growth-stock multiples compress materially. Trade barriers ratchet up as advanced economies use sanctions as crisis-response tool; geopolitical flashpoints gain intensity beyond the Middle East. The US powerhouse persists but its outperformance becomes specifically about benefiting from the inflation differential — sectoral, not broad-based. Resolution comes within 5–7 years either via deep recession forcing inflation back down or via successful re-anchoring after structural adjustment.

## Macro picture

Global growth slows to 1.5–2.0% through the recession period; advanced economies contract 0.5% in year 2. Headline inflation peaks at 5.5–6% in year 2 then settles around 4–5% through the entrenchment phase, fading toward 3.5% in the resolution phase. Nominal cash rates in advanced economies 5–7%; real rates 2.5%. USD strong on rate-differential and safe-haven flows; commodity currencies bifurcate; some EM currency crises in stressed sovereigns.

## US powerhouse stance

The US powerhouse persists structurally but its outperformance becomes specifically about US firms benefiting from the inflation differential — commodity-exposed names, financials with wide NIM, defensive-pricing-power names. Other economies don't get the same lift. The US recession is milder than Europe's; the US recovers faster.

## Climate policy posture

Stagnant or backsliding. Cost-of-living crisis crushes climate political capital further than Muddle Through. Carbon-pricing rollbacks accelerate; renewable subsidies cut to fund inflation-relief programs; transition capex slows under high cost of capital. Aggregate emissions trajectory drifts upward. The disorderly path is reinforced. Climate-policy fatigue becomes chronic.

## AI and productivity

AI investment continues (deficit-funded, US-led, doesn't stop) but the financing environment compresses marginal capex; well-capitalised firms continue AI investment, poorly-capitalised firms fall behind. Productivity dispersion intensifies. Growth-stock multiples compress materially as discount rates rise. Aggregate TFP growth around 0.5%, similar to AI Productivity Lag in magnitude but in a much harsher macro environment.

## Geopolitics and trade

Energy-driven contestation intensifies. Middle East flashpoints active and unresolved. Other regional flashpoints (Taiwan, Russia-NATO) opportunistically gain intensity. Sanctions used aggressively as crisis-response tool. Tariffs and export controls ratchet up. Energy-product embargoes proliferate. Resource nationalism intensifying as energy-exporting states leverage supply position.

## Demographic response

Harder than Muddle Through because the cost of capital is higher, slowing the robotics / automation capex that would address it. China presses on regardless (strategic capital is non-economic). US politicises immigration further. Europe loses both fronts. Australia faces sharp wage pressure on top of the recession-driven labour-market loosening.

## Sectoral picture

Sharp dispersion. Energy / commodities / commodity-currency exposures outperform; financials with wide NIM benefit through the early phase before credit losses bite; defensive pricing-power names hold up. Growth / cyclicals / consumer-discretionary underperform. Banks face NIM tailwind early, credit-quality stress mid-scenario. Mining sees energy-input cost spike but commodity-price support partly offsets.

## How the world becomes Stagflation Persists

The trigger is most likely Middle East escalation deepening through the year — Strait of Hormuz disruption, energy infrastructure damage, oil sustained above USD 110. The second-round dynamics that distinguish this scenario from Muddle Through depend on inflation expectations breaking above 3% with credibility loss for central banks. Once that happens — and the experience of 2021–22 suggests it can happen surprisingly fast — wage settlements in services accelerate and the entrenchment phase begins.

## What would invalidate this scenario

Energy-price spike fading without second-round transmission (would shift toward Muddle Through). Credible central-bank action re-anchoring inflation expectations (would shift toward Muddle Through or Orderly Convergence). Recession deeper than expected with rapid disinflation (would shift toward a deflationary case not in the current set).

## Implications for IPL, CSL, WBC (brief)

**IPL** — margin-compression case. Input costs rise sharply (ammonia, gas, transport); supplier-side pricing power is moderate so pass-through is incomplete; mining-customer capex slows under credit-tightening; long-term gas contracts soften but don't eliminate the cost-shock. Net: negative EBIT margin trajectory, modestly negative on revenue, materially negative on multiples.

**CSL** — mixed-to-negative. Healthcare spend nominal-stable but real-terms compressed; plasma-collection labour costs rise sharply; pipeline-NPV multiples compress as real rates rise and growth-stock premium evaporates. Earnings hold up but multiples compress materially.

**WBC** — the most interesting case for the framework. NIM benefits significantly from sustained higher rates (asset-side reprices faster than liability-side; net interest margin widens). Loan growth slows under restrictive financial conditions. **But credit losses rise materially** as recession bites and unemployment moves up; specifically, the SME / commercial book takes the hit, with mortgage-book pressure from variable-rate stress. The NIM tailwind and the credit-cycle headwind work against each other. Net outcome depends critically on the depth and length of the recession.
